## FIGHT 1 — Ilia Topuria vs. Justin Gaethje (Lightweight Title Unification, 5 rounds) — MAIN EVENT

### Ilia Topuria — 17-0 · age 29 · 5'7" / 69" reach · orthodox · BJJ black belt
- **The full picture:** Undefeated two-division champion. 7 KO / 8 sub / 2 dec (~88% finish rate; only 2 of 17 fights reached the judges). Last four wins: **KO Oliveira (R1), KO Holloway (R3), KO Volkanovski (R2), dec. Emmett** — a murderer's row, three of them former/reigning champions finished by strikes.
- **Stats:** ~4.8 sig strikes/min, ~64% striking defense, **~94% takedown defense**, ~1.25 knockdowns/15min. Elite, two-way threat: real one-punch right hand AND a genuine submission game (he out-grappled elite grappler Bryce Mitchell).
- **Pros vs. Gaethje:** Faster, younger (by 8 years), undefeated, the harder *clean* puncher, far better grappling, and — critically — **he finished both common opponents (Holloway, Oliveira) that Gaethje LOST to** (Holloway KO'd Gaethje; Oliveira submitted Gaethje). Now at his natural 155 (escaped the brutal 145 cut), power fully intact (R1 KO of Oliveira in LW debut). Elite fight IQ — lost round 1 to Volkanovski and still KO'd him in round 2.
- **Cons / risks:** **~12-month layoff** (his longest — a voluntary sabbatical amid a personal/legal "extortion" saga, not injury) → ring rust is the real question. Shorter height and reach than Gaethje. He is **hittable** (absorbs ~3.8/min, was dropped once by Jai Herbert's head kick). **Limited championship-round mileage** — only one 5-round fight ever (Emmett). Left his 13-year gym (Climent Club) for his own smaller Madrid/Miami "Topuria Team."

### Justin Gaethje — 27-5 · age 37 · 5'11" / 70" reach · orthodox · D1 All-American wrestler
- **The full picture:** 20 KO wins, highest bonus rate in UFC history, interim LW champ. Beat Pimblett (UD, Jan 2026) and Fiziev to earn this. Losses only to elite (Khabib, Oliveira, Alvarez, Poirier, Holloway).
- **Stats:** ~6.5–7.4 sig strikes/min and elite ~60% accuracy, but **absorbs ~7/min with only ~51% striking defense** — he wins damage races, he doesn't avoid damage. Signature: fight-altering **leg kicks**. Rarely uses his wrestling.
- **Pros vs. Topuria:** Devastating leg kicks that could compromise a rusty Topuria's mobility; one-shot power retained late (head-kick KO of Poirier, knockdowns of Pimblett); proven 5-round cardio and toughness; full camp under Trevor Wittman with venue-specific heat conditioning; nothing-to-lose "last shot at undisputed gold" motivation.
- **Cons / risks:** **37 with enormous war-mileage**; KO'd by Holloway at UFC 300 (last-second) and reportedly dropped in several recent fights — durability is slipping. Low striking defense vs. the cleanest puncher he'll have faced. Public divorce as a possible off-mat distraction.

### Verdict
The five candidate calls: **Topuria wins ~69%** · Gaethje wins ~26% · doesn't-go-distance ~55% · goes-distance ~43% · draw ~1%.
**PICK: Ilia Topuria wins (any method) — 69%.** It beats "doesn't go the distance" (~55%) because Topuria's edge is *complete*, not just finishing — if Gaethje's chin and heart drag it to the scorecards (very plausible given his toughness and Topuria's one 5-round-decision precedent vs. Emmett), Topuria still wins the decision. The shared-opponent math (he finished the two men who beat Gaethje), the youth/speed/grappling edges, and the move to a healthy weight outweigh Gaethje's leg kicks and the rust factor. **Biggest risk to the pick:** ring rust + a Gaethje leg-kick/early-power start landing before Topuria finds timing.

## FIGHT 2 — Alex Pereira vs. Ciryl Gane (Interim Heavyweight Title, 5 rounds) — CO-MAIN

*(Interim belt because undisputed champ Tom Aspinall is recovering from a double-eye injury from the UFC 321 No Contest — vs. Gane.)*

### Alex Pereira — 13-3 (11 KO) · age 38 · 6'4" / 79" · orthodox · weighed 251
- Two-division champ chasing a **historic third-division title**, moving UP from light heavyweight (~+46 lb body transformation). Coming off an 80-second R1 KO revenge of Ankalaev. Best one-shot striker in MMA — the left hook ends nights (Adesanya, Procházka, Hill, Rountree, Ankalaev).
- **Pros:** The single most dangerous weapon in the cage (power transfers up; KO'd everyone he's cleanly landed on); improved TDD (~74–79%); proven big-stage killer.
- **Cons:** Moves **linearly** and struggles to cut off rangy lateral movers; **cardio at 251 lb over 5 rounds is completely unproven** (the 2025 Ankalaev decision exposed a fade — he admitted being "~40%"); gives up 2" of reach; 38 years old debuting at a new, heavier division.

### Ciryl Gane — 13-2 (1 NC) · age 36 · 6'5" / 81" · orthodox · weighed 248
- Natural heavyweight, elite-movement technical striker (former Muay Thai 13-0). Best defensive numbers in the division (~2.3 absorbed/min, ~61% defense, 61% accuracy). Never been KO'd.
- **Pros:** The **mover archetype that troubles Pereira** — footwork, feints, volume, +2" reach; great HW cardio (multiple 25-min fights); his historic weakness (takedown defense ~47%) is **largely irrelevant** here because Pereira is a kickboxer, not a wrestler; he even teased using his *own* wrestling. Full opponent-specific camp (sparred Pereira's kickboxing conqueror Artem Vakhitov + Glory champ Mory Kromah).
- **Cons:** Chin was visibly buzzed by Tuivasa; **0-2 in title fights** (Ngannou, Jones) with shaky big-moment composure; recent form unconvincing (disputed Volkov split, sub-1-round Aspinall NC); ~7.5-month layoff.

### Verdict
This is the card's true coin-flip on *who wins*: **Pereira ~49% · Gane ~41% · draw ~2%.** But the **distance** question is far clearer. Pereira finishes ~85% of his wins with the best one-shot power in the sport; Gane has real KO power too and a chin that's been hurt; and a tiring Pereira late is *also* a finish risk (gets stopped or stops). **Does NOT go the distance ~60%** vs. goes-the-distance ~40%.
**PICK: Does NOT go the distance — 60%.** It's the highest-probability single call because it captures the most likely events from *both* men simultaneously, where naming a winner is barely better than a coin flip. **Secondary lean if forced to name a winner: Pereira** (the power edge is the great equalizer). **Biggest risk:** Gane flawlessly stick-and-moves for 25 minutes and steals a decision — exactly the fight that beats Pereira's profile.

## FIGHT 3 — Sean O'Malley vs. Aiemann Zahabi (Bantamweight, 3 rounds)

### Sean O'Malley — ~18-3 (1 NC) · age 31 · 5'11" / 72" · switch stance
- Former champ rebuilding after two losses to Merab Dvalishvili; bounced back with a decision over Song Yadong (Jan 2026), healthy after Oct 2025 hip surgery. Elite rangy power-striker: ~6+ sig strikes/min, ~1.0 knockdowns/min, 60%+ accuracy, **never cleanly KO'd**.
- **Pros vs. Zahabi:** ~3.5" reach + 3" height + 7 years of youth; far more power and volume; switch-stance unpredictability. **Crucially, his ONLY proven weakness — takedown defense (~60%) — is neutralized**, because Zahabi is a *defensive* grappler who almost never shoots (~12–16% TD accuracy). This becomes a pure kickboxing match, O'Malley's best-case scenario. Reportedly reset mindset (cut social media/gaming/marijuana).
- **Cons:** Can be lulled into low-output "pot-shot" lulls (R2 vs. Song); Zahabi's elite defense + Tristar game-planning could steal rounds in a 3-round fight if O'Malley hunts the finish too patiently.

### Aiemann Zahabi — 14-2 · age 38 · 5'8" / 68" · orthodox · Tristar (brother Firas)
- Career-best run (a reported 7-fight streak; decision wins over **José Aldo** and **Marlon Vera**, KO of Aoriqileng). Elite ~69% striking defense, ~84% TDD, durable, high fight IQ, great cardio. Counter-striker with **no real power**.
- **Pros:** Hard to finish (one career KO loss), comfortable in a 15-minute technical grind, Tristar blueprint to frustrate a star, fearless underdog.
- **Cons:** 38, smaller, slower, must out-point a longer/harder-hitting younger man **with no takedown threat and no power to earn respect**. Common opponent Vera: O'Malley beat Vera far more decisively (50-45 level) than Zahabi (split).

### Verdict
**O'Malley wins ~72%** · Zahabi wins ~24% · doesn't-go-distance ~50% · goes-distance ~49% · draw ~1%.
**PICK: Sean O'Malley wins (any method) — 72% (highest-confidence play on the card).** The decisive variable — wrestling pressure — is the exact tool that beat O'Malley twice, and Zahabi simply doesn't bring it. Strip out grappling and every physical and skill advantage in a pure striking match favors O'Malley; the call wins by KO **or** decision. **Biggest risk:** Zahabi's defense/durability forces an ugly, close decision he edges with activity.

## FIGHT 4 — Josh Hokit vs. Derrick Lewis (Heavyweight, 3 rounds)

- Undefeated ex-49ers fullback and **NCAA D1 All-American wrestler**; rocketed up in ~8 months with two R1 UFC finishes and a 15-minute Fight-of-the-Year decision over Curtis Blaydes. Elite athleticism, wrestling base, high pace, proven cardio (the Blaydes war), 8-of-9 finish rate.

- **Cons:** **Hittable (44% striking defense)** vs. the hardest puncher in UFC history; **~2-month turnaround** after a brutal war (Lewis publicly mocked it); **head coach Greg Jackson not in his corner**; 34 lb lighter; chin untested vs. elite one-shot power; only 9 pro fights.

### Derrick Lewis — 29-13 (1 NC) · age 41 · 6'3" / 79" · orthodox · weighed 265
- **UFC all-time KO leader (16).** ~83% of wins by KO. The great equalizer: one shot ends anyone. But coming off a R2 TKO loss (Cortes-Acosta), tied for most UFC KO *losses* (8), famously terrible cardio (fades hard after R1), porous striking defense (~40%), middling TDD, and submission-prone. On the card by President Trump's personal request; says it's the hardest he's ever trained.
- **Pros:** Puncher's chance is *always* live (~30–35%), especially round 1; size/power/experience; massive motivation.
- **Cons:** 41, declining chin, awful gas tank, beatable by wrestling/pressure — exactly Hokit's game.

### Verdict
**Hokit wins ~61%** · Lewis wins ~34% · doesn't-go-distance ~62% · goes-distance ~38% · draw ~1%.
**PICK: Josh Hokit wins (any method) — 61%.** Youth, cardio, and a wrestling style tailor-made to exploit Lewis's two biggest holes (gas tank + takedown defense) outweigh Lewis's punchers' chance. "Doesn't go the distance" is nearly as likely (~62%) and a fair alternative, but it pays on a Lewis KO I rate clearly less probable. **Biggest risk (and it's a real one at heavyweight):** Hokit gets hit on a wrestling entry early and Lewis lands the one bomb. Lewis is the single most dangerous "upset" threat on the card.

## FIGHT 5 — Mauricio Ruffy vs. Michael Chandler (Lightweight, 3 rounds)

- **Cons:** **Documented grappling hole** — submitted by Benoît Saint-Denis (Sep 2025) who wrestled him down at will; can't wrestle himself; cardio unproven deep; thin elite résumé; fights with hands low.

### Michael Chandler — 23-10 (2-5 UFC) · age 40 · 5'8" / 71"
- Explosive former Bellator champ and D1 wrestler, but on a **3-fight skid**, **last win May 2022** (4+ years), 0-3 since (Pimblett TKO, Oliveira dec, Poirier sub). Fades late, now owns 5 KO losses.
- **Pros:** His **wrestling is the one tool that beats Ruffy** (the Saint-Denis blueprint); explosive R1 power.
- **Cons:** 40, eroded (61% TDD, 41% TD accuracy), gives up length, fades and gets finished late, ~14-month layoff.

### Verdict
**Ruffy wins ~61%** · Chandler wins ~33% · doesn't-go-distance ~64% · goes-distance ~30% · draw <2%.
**PICK: Mauricio Ruffy wins (any method) — 61%.** Youth, length, striking variety and Chandler's steep decline make the younger man the most likely winner — most probably a R1–R2 KO/TKO. "Does not go the distance" (~64%) is a defensible outcome-agnostic hedge, but it also pays on a Chandler win I rate much lower. **Biggest risk:** Chandler lands the early overhand right OR drags Ruffy down and reprises the Saint-Denis grappling beatdown before his own tank empties (~33%).

- **Cons:** **First loss exposed him** — Reinier de Ridder TKO'd him with a body/liver knee and he "imploded" when hurt; clinch-striking and body durability are flags; cardio largely untested past R3; carries pressure to prove the rebound is real.

### Kyle Daukaus — 17-4 (1 NC) · age 33 · 6'2" / 76" · southpaw · "The D'Arce Knight"
- Submission specialist (12 sub wins), on a 6-fight overall win streak with two quick UFC finishes since returning (KO of Michel Pereira in 43s, D'Arce of Meerschaert in 50s). Strong TDD (83%).
- **Pros:** Dangerous front-headlock/D'Arce game **specifically against a wrestler who shoots** — the one realistic path to an upset; reach edge; nothing-to-lose motivation.
- **Cons:** Porous striking defense (43%), absorbs more than he lands, has been quickly KO'd by power strikers (Dolidze, Anders); Nickal's wrestling is a tier above.

### Verdict
**Nickal wins ~62%** · Daukaus wins ~33% · doesn't-go-distance ~65% · goes-distance ~34% · draw ~1%.
**PICK: Bo Nickal wins (any method) — 62% (revised down from 68%).** His wrestling and athleticism should still let him control position and win by ground-and-pound TKO, submission, or decision. But here is the under-weighted, non-consensus point: **Nickal's only loss (Reinier de Ridder) came against a long, scramble-heavy submission grappler with body striking — and Daukaus's skill set rhymes with that exact archetype** (D'Arce-choke specialist, +1" reach, dangerous off his back, two straight first-round UFC finishes, new-father "loose veteran" psychology). Combined with Nickal's proven body-shot vulnerability and the hype-pressure he carries on this stage, this is a coin-flip-tier leg, not a safe one. **"Does NOT go the distance" (~65%) is the single highest-probability call** — both men finish *and* both have a clear way to be finished — and is the better play if you don't need to name the winner. I keep "Nickal wins" as the directional pick only because his control-to-decision path (à la Paul Craig) keeps "no distance" from dominating it. **Biggest risk:** Daukaus catches a careless Nickal shot/scramble in a D'Arce, or buckles the exposed-when-hurt Nickal — the most dangerous submission threat Nickal has faced since the man who already beat him.

### Steve Garcia — 19-5 (6-0 at FW) · age 34 · 6'0" / 75" · southpaw · Jackson-Wink

## OUT-OF-THE-BOX EDGE FACTORS — THE STRICT READ (what the consensus misses)

This is the layer that separates a precise prediction from a popular one: the camp politics, injuries, psychology, personal lives, and the unprecedented venue. Each point below is weighted for what it actually does to the fight.

### Card-wide environmental edges (affect everyone, weighted by slot)
- **The venue is a weapon and a wildcard.** First-ever outdoor White House South Lawn card, evening, ~80°F→mid-60s with humidity, ~30–40% storm chance and a **lightning-evacuation protocol** that could cause mid-card delays. Delays wreck warm-up timing and favor calm veterans over tightly-wound fighters. Heat punishes the cardio-suspect — most relevant to the heavier/earlier bouts.
- **The Greg Jackson absence is a TWO-FIGHT story almost nobody is pricing in.** Jackson-Wink's Greg Jackson is **NOT cornering either Josh Hokit or Steve Garcia** (personal/family reasons). Losing your lead strategist for the biggest fight of your life — against, respectively, the hardest puncher in UFC history and the best opponent you've ever faced — is a concrete, compounding negative for *both* underdogs-in-spirit. This is my single favorite under-weighted edge on the card.
- **Partisan, political, once-in-history crowd + Trump's 80th birthday.** Showmen and invited favorites feed on it (Topuria, O'Malley, Lewis — personally requested by Trump). The raw/younger fighters carrying hype-pressure (Hokit, Nickal) are the ones it can tighten.

### Fight-by-fight contrarian points
1. **Topuria–Gaethje — the quick KO is the trap bet.** Topuria publicly predicted a ~2-minute finish; the *strict* read says the opposite is more likely. He's coming off his **longest layoff (~12 months) spent inside a brutal personal saga** (breakup/custody, an alleged extortion/false-accusation ordeal) AND he **left the Climent Club after 13 years** for his own small team — losing camp infrastructure and accountability right before the biggest night of his life. Meanwhile **Gaethje's camp ran an "extreme" sauna heat-adaptation protocol specifically for these outdoor conditions** — a real, specific edge in humidity over five rounds, where Topuria's deep-water cardio is essentially unproven (one career 5-rounder). **Net:** Topuria still wins on talent, youth, clean power and the shared-opponent math — but it is more likely to be *competitive and long* (decision or late finish) than the early blowout people expect, and Gaethje's genuine live window is rounds 4–5 with leg kicks + heat. Confidence trimmed 69→67.
2. **Pereira–Gane — Gane is the disrespected dog, and the heat is on Poatan.** Everyone is buying the left hook. The overlooked truth: **Pereira added ~46 lbs of fresh mass at 38 and has zero proven 5-round heavyweight cardio**, and an outdoor humid night is the worst possible environment for a freshly-bulked body to gas. Gane is the *natural* big man, elite mover, +2" reach, great gas tank, and **imported Pereira's only kickboxing conqueror (Artem Vakhitov) plus a current Glory champ to mirror him**. The fight is genuinely bimodal: **early Pereira KO, or a late Gane decision/finish as Poatan tires.** That's exactly why "does NOT go the distance" is the high-confidence call — but if you must name a winner, **Gane is far more live than the consensus admits.**
3. **O'Malley–Zahabi — bolder than "easy decision": a real finish is on the table.** The consensus sees a points win. The sharp angle: **O'Malley has reset his life** (cut social media, gaming, marijuana; "healthy family life") after a near-retirement scare — a re-focused, motivated star. Zahabi must walk *forward into O'Malley's counter right hand* with **no power to earn respect and no wrestling threat**, and at 38 his chin has shown cracks (rocked by Vera and Aldo recently). This is the rare matchup where O'Malley's lone weakness (wrestling) is fully removed. Highest-confidence leg on the card, with live finish upside.
4. **Hokit–Lewis — the card's #1 upset spot (and I trimmed Hokit to 58%).** The chalk "young athlete drowns the old man" ignores three stacked negatives: **(a) no Greg Jackson in the corner**; **(b) Winkeljohn is publicly nervous Hokit will try to "entertain"** — i.e., stand and bang with the all-time KO leader instead of wrestling; **(c) a ~2-month turnaround after a 354-strike war in which he was wobbled repeatedly**, with a 44% striking defense and an untested chin. Add the weigh-in circus (vomiting "in character") = emotional, reckless energy. Hokit *should* win by wrestling and cardio — but the single most likely shocker of the night is **Derrick Lewis landing one bomb early** on a hyped, under-coached, hittable prospect who likes to brawl. This is a coin-flip dressed as a layup.
5. **Ruffy–Chandler — the upset key is Chandler's ego, not his odds.** Chandler's *only* path is wrestling — and it's the exact blueprint (grappling pressure) that **already finished Ruffy via submission (Saint-Denis)**; Chandler is a far better wrestler than Saint-Denis. The reason I still pick Ruffy: **Chandler historically refuses to wrestle**, preferring wars (pride/legacy), which walks him onto Ruffy's power and a chin that already owns 5 KO losses at age 40. Also monitor **Ruffy's camp friction with Fighting Nerds** (cornering instability around his Volkanovski-camp alignment). If Chandler fights his age and wrestles, he's live; if he brawls (his nature), Ruffy KO.
6. **Nickal–Daukaus — see revised verdict above:** the de Ridder template makes Daukaus the most dangerous "rhyme" opponent for Nickal's one proven weakness; coin-flip-tier, Nickal trimmed to 62%.
7. **Lopes–Garcia — Lopes is more compromised than his ranking suggests.** Hidden physical stressors: **Lopes reportedly broke BOTH feet in his last fight (~4.5 months ago), took this on short notice (found out from TV), and weighed in TWICE on fight week** (146 for Garcia + 154 as the lightweight main-event backup) — a bizarre double weight/rehydration cycle and split focus that can quietly sap pressure and cardio. On the other side, **Garcia is missing Greg Jackson AND publicly fixated on the outdoor conditions** (a mental yellow flag), and has never faced anyone near Lopes's level. Lopes's experience, grappling and never-finished-in-the-UFC durability still edge it — but if Garcia lands his left hand early, this is a very live upset. Lowest-confidence leg (55%).

### The boldest non-consensus calls to take away

- **Gane is a live underdog**, not a stepping stone — the heat and his cardio/movement are real.
- **Hokit–Lewis is the most likely upset on the card** (Lewis KO), driven by Hokit's missing coach + reckless tendency.
- **O'Malley could *finish* Zahabi**, not just out-point him.
- **Nickal is a coin flip, not a showcase** — Daukaus is the de Ridder archetype.
